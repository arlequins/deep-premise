# Unseen Order — three-party concept review

Status: REOPENED. The player rejected the selected salvage concept as too familiar and asked for defense alternatives. Implementation remains paused. The earlier votes below are historical, not current approval. The caravan and colony sketches are not the selected game and are not connected to the active scene.

## What the player rejected

The conversation prototype did not provide a productive early goal, readable individual activity, or satisfying visible growth. Repeated dialogue choices and required typing were tiring. Conventional building placement also failed the novelty requirement. Keep the independent deterministic core, bilingual presentation, local playtest consent, and exact-state diagnostics; reconsider the actual activity loop.

The new target is visible autonomous work, an immediate physical intervention, growth in capability, systemic surprise with discoverable causes, and roguelike variation. The player additionally requested three-party voting as a game mechanic.

## Discussants and method

- Lead reviewer: integrated the player's feedback and independently proposed Triad Salvage.
- Play reviewer: a GPT-5.6 Luna agent, evaluating the first ninety seconds, tactile agency, and legibility.
- World reviewer: a separate GPT-5.6 Luna agent, evaluating causal surprise, simulation depth, voting, and a feasible initial slice.

Both agents first proposed concepts independently. They then received the other proposals and the lead reviewer's objections, and submitted rebuttals and final votes. These are design judgments, not playtest results.

## Candidates

| Candidate | Productive player activity | What grows | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flow Country | Redirect water, herds, spores and temporary expedition interventions | Ecological corridors and usable patches | Too many coupled systems can obscure the first cause and effect |
| Driftworks | Tether, tow, detach, recover and reuse drifting wreck hardware | The capabilities and working topology of a mobile flotilla | May become ordinary construction with a different skin |
| Axiom Expedition | Set survey mandates, assign observers and organize recovery routes | Reach, evidence and learned operating methods | Early payoff may depend on abstract planning and an atlas |
| Protocol Seed | Seed and modify inherited autonomous routines | A self-organizing ecology of behavior | Risks becoming an opaque screensaver |
| Triad Salvage | Recover objects, sample them, select processing workflows and equip a team | Reliable processing knowledge and physical equipment | Unknown-object decisions could become repetitive menu puzzles |

The previously proposed automatic caravan routes were also challenged: a clear logistics loop alone is not enough to establish the distinctive worldview.

## Actual discussion

The play reviewer initially favored Flow Country for novelty and non-building growth. After the first-minute and scope challenge, this reviewer preferred Driftworks plus Triad Salvage, with evidence attached directly to objects rather than a separate atlas interface.

The world reviewer initially favored Axiom Expedition for its hidden-rule structure. After comparing the first physical payoff, this reviewer voted for Driftworks plus Triad Salvage. The condition was explicit: recovered hardware must alter a useful capability within five minutes, or the concept loses its advantage.

The lead reviewer favored Triad Salvage and adopted Driftworks' physical recovery interaction. The lead rejected a broad ecology simulation and mandatory early vote dialogs, because both could recreate the player's original confusion and click fatigue.

## Final concept vote

| Discussant | Final vote | Deciding reason |
|---|---|---|
| Play reviewer | Driftworks + Triad Salvage, with object-based evidence | Tethering and recovering a physical object makes agency immediately legible |
| World reviewer | Driftworks + Triad Salvage | One small recovery loop can expose consistent hidden rules without a large simulation stack |
| Lead reviewer | Driftworks + Triad Salvage | Productive work and visible capability growth can precede uncertainty and social disagreement |

Selected: **a mobile salvage community**. Three votes support this specific synthesis, not an unrestricted merger of all proposed systems.

## Core loop

Tether a visible object -> named crew recover it -> inspect or process it -> reuse working hardware -> reach a harder recovery target.

Growth is functional rather than numerical alone: a recovered arm changes what can be hauled; a separator changes which material can be recovered; a stabilizer makes a different operation possible. These are working objects with observable effects, not a catalogue of houses to place.

The first minute contains routine successful work. The first exceptional object introduces uncertainty only after the player has seen one dependable chain complete.

## Three-party voting: disagreement and decision

The play reviewer favored **player + two NPC voters** to preserve direct agency. The world reviewer favored **three NPC voters**, with the player controlling the agenda, evidence, jobs and operational limits. The lead favors three NPC voters, making this a 2–1 design preference rather than a unanimous conclusion.

Prototype rules:

- Ordinary known recovery, carrying and processing follow the player's work orders without voting.
- A vote is triggered only by a consequential proposal involving a risky unknown object.
- Three named residents vote from their actual observations, remembered evidence and current needs. No arbitrary random ballot and no live LLM is required.
- The player sets the proposed operation, assigns inspection work, routes samples, and manages available resources. These actions change the voters' information rather than purchasing a favorable vote.
- Start with a binary proposal: proceed or hold. Three voters does not require three alternatives. A simple majority prevents a routine 1–1–1 deadlock from becoming the core activity.
- A held object occupies only its own work slot; ordinary production continues. The player can obtain new evidence or leave that object aside.
- Show each person's vote and the evidence icon or need that led to it beside their name. Do not explain the hidden world rule or display omniscient truth scores.
- A majority chooses the action. It does not make a belief physically true. Several people may share the same bad source.
- Do not add a mandatory dialogue exchange, an always-on council window, or repeated appeals after an unchanged vote.

The most important agency test is whether a player can understand an alternative useful action immediately after a held proposal. If not, the voting model needs revision, including reconsidering the player-held seat.

## First playable scope

- One small recovery area and one mobile working platform.
- Three clearly identifiable crew members with visible current jobs.
- Three recoverable object types, one dependable processing chain and one unknown interaction.
- One physical equipment upgrade that changes the next available operation.
- One consequential three-person vote with different information sources.
- One deterministic hazard and a few seeded starting differences.
- Per-expedition equipment or doctrine tradeoffs; preserve a failed run and permit a fresh seeded start.
- Detailed local actions, evidence, ballots, reasoning inputs, consequences and state replay; no credentials in logs.
- No player chat input, house placement, broad ecosystem, combat system, separate lore atlas, or generated-dialogue dependency in this first slice.

## Early success criteria

1. Within ten seconds, the player can identify a manipulable recovery target.
2. Within thirty seconds of the first order, an observable physical result occurs.
3. Within ninety seconds, recovered material becomes useful without a lore explanation.
4. Within five minutes, equipment changes what the crew can do.
5. At every moment, crew identity, current task, target and blocked reason are readable.
6. The first surprising outcome follows stored causes and leaves evidence the player could discover.
7. A held council proposal never freezes all productive activity.

Use the player's actual play and F8 markers to judge these conditions. Approval by three design discussants does not establish that the game is fun.

## Defense reconsideration

Both Luna reviewers independently preferred protecting a continuously operating physical process against threats that learn visible routines. Their alternatives were shepherding a herd or protecting a bloom. The lead requires visible scouting, pursuit and attacks, not only inspection menus or production statistics. A promising hook is controlling what enemy scouts actually learn: intercept an observer, alter a route, or let it escape after seeing a decoy. Enemy learning must use finite witnessed evidence, never omniscient counters or arbitrary punishment for repetition. Ordinary production continues; triad votes apply to consequential commitments only. This is a new proposal for discussion, not authorization to resume implementation.
